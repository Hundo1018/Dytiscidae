# Repair R3, result: the default lift and drag from two measured thin sections

2026-11-17. Frozen text: `PREREG_R3_table.md` (`151a592`). Checks:
`r3_check.py` -> `r3_results.json`; `tests/test_physics.py
::test_the_table_law_reads_two_measured_thin_sections`; the battery
(`run.py`) and the mirrors' suites, below.

## What was built

`fluid.table_coefficients` (`FluidSolver.stall_law = "table"`): a lifting
strip with no section of its own reads the UIUC flat plate (camber 0) and
Goe 417a (camber 5.78%, from the tunnel model's outline), linear in camber
between them at the same angle towards the convex side, the arc's values
held past 5.78%. The tables are baked on a uniform grid
(`physics/thin_plate_tables.py`, generated by `tools/bake_thin_plate.py`
from the csv files, 0.25 deg, five Reynolds numbers, -25..30 deg) so the
numpy solver, the Mojo kernel and the device path read the same numbers.
`SectionPolar.coefficients` was split (`table_part`) so the baked Goe 417a
rows are the section's own arithmetic; its results are unchanged.

## Built wrong the first time (two faults, repaired before the read below)

- The flat plate's four lift runs were interpolated in file-name order
  (100k, 120k, 60k, 80k), not by Reynolds number: T1's flat row read 0.129.
- A baked row stopped at the lift run's last angle, so between there and 30
  deg the drag held its last value where the section's goes on as
  `cl tan(alpha)`: T2's drag rows read 0.065-0.070. The grid now runs to
  `SectionPolar.PLATE_BY`.

No tolerance and no prediction was changed.

What the failures are:

- **T1, 0.073.** Not the grid. `SectionPolar` joins the drag run's own lift
  (which ends at 11.65 deg reading 1.367) to the lift run (1.327 at the
  same angle) across 6 deg, so just past the peak the law is up to 0.07
  above the lift run, and below the drag run's negative end it goes to the
  plate where the lift run goes on to -5 deg. I wrote the check against the
  lift run alone and should not have. It is the flapper line's section as
  well (W5-W8 flew it).
- **T7 oddness.** `np.arange(-90, 90.001, 0.1)` is not symmetric in the
  last bit; at exactly mirrored angles the difference is 0.0. The check's
  grid, not the law. The suite's test uses mirrored angles.
- **T3 at 25-30 deg: the real one.** Point by point (r3_check prints them):
  from 5 to 20 deg the table law is within 0.12 of the wings (the legacy
  law is 0.2-0.48 high at 12-15 deg for aspect ratio 3-5 and at 20 deg
  for 2, where it has not stalled yet, and 0.14-0.15 low at 20 deg for
  3-5). At 25-30 deg the table law is 0.03-0.29 high in lift and
  0.03-0.19 high in drag, where the legacy law, already on the separated
  plate, is within 0.17 and 0.07. The cause is the flat plate's
  two-dimensional run itself: it rises from 0.81-0.92 at 15 deg to
  1.18-1.24 at 24, and finite flat wings of aspect ratio 2-5 do not
  (0.67-0.81 from 12 to 30 deg). The drag error is the lift error times
  tan(alpha): the wings' own CD is their CL tan(alpha) within 0.01-0.02 at
  all six points, so the normal-force rule for the flat plate's drag holds
  on them.
- **T4.** Two builds of nearly one camber (5.65% and 5.78%, 5.0% and 3.4%
  thick) differ by up to 0.12 in lift past 8 deg and 0.16 near zero
  incidence at Re 60k. That is the scatter the interpolation cannot be
  better than.

## What this does and does not establish

The law is the measurements where they were made, and the three backends
can share it. It is closer than the legacy law to finite flat wings up to
20 deg and to a measured cambered section everywhere up to its stall (the
legacy law gives a 5.8% arc CL 0.73 at zero incidence where the tunnel
read 0.02-0.41, and a profile drag of 0.008 where it read 0.019-0.041).

It is worse than the legacy law for a flat wing between 25 and 30 deg,
by up to 0.29 in CL. Whether the two-dimensional flat-plate run above 15
deg is right for a wing at all is open: the run is the only tabulated
flat-plate section data found, it was taken between splitter plates with a
12 in chord, and no drag was measured with it. Not repaired here: any cut
of that run chosen now would be chosen with Ananda's points in view.

Also open, as frozen: nothing between 0 and 5.78% camber was measured;
nothing below Re 60k (held); static tables, no dynamic stall.
