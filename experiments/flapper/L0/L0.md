# L0, pass 1 (2026-11-15): does the built machine close on paper?

Plan: `../PLAN.md` (draft 3, approved 2026-11-15). Arithmetic:
`closure.py` in this directory (`.venv/bin/python experiments/flapper/L0/closure.py`,
no third-party package); its full output is the table every number below is
read from. Inputs are reports A11, A13 and A14; none of them is a measurement
made here. Numbers marked TYPED are nobody's measurement and are swept.

This is paper. A row that "closes" has not been shown to work; it has not
been shown impossible with the numbers in hand. A row that does not close
names what would have to be true.

## What was computed

Mass closes when battery + drive + wings + structure + fixed electronics
equal the all-up mass. Battery: 15 minutes at the measured 122-175 W/kg of
forward flapping flight. Drive: E-Flap's 14% of mass, scaled up with the
peak power a standing take-off asks. Wings: 187 g/m2. Structure: 20-30% of
mass (TYPED). Fixed: 80 g (TYPED).

A standing take-off is sized on the one machine that has done it at this
scale, RoboFalcon 2.0 (0.8 kg, 7 Hz, 400 W, tethered): it implies a mean
lift coefficient of 1.04 in the blade-element identity of A11 (inside the
0.98-1.28 found there for a 16 g hoverer, which is the only check this
calibration has) and an electrical peak 20 times the ideal induced power.
Both are carried to other wing loadings **unchanged, which is an assumption**.

## Result 1: the lever is wing loading, not the power source

36 of 36 combinations close on mass, between 0.20 and 0.76 kg. What separates
them is the frequency a standing take-off needs:

| wing loading | all-up mass | span | take-off frequency | against a 5.5 Hz drive | peak power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.64 kg/m2 (RoboFalcon's) | 0.20-0.71 kg | 0.58-1.08 m | 7.7-14.5 Hz | 0.38-0.71 | 522 W/kg |
| 2.5 kg/m2 | 0.20-0.66 kg | 0.69-1.26 m | 5.5-10.1 Hz | 0.55-1.00 | 433 W/kg |
| 1.78 kg/m2 (Kestrel's) | 0.21-0.76 kg | 0.83-1.60 m | 3.7-7.0 Hz | 0.78-1.50 | 365 W/kg |

- A heavier machine with large wings is easier to lift off than a light one
  with small wings: the frequency falls with wing length. The lightest
  machines (0.2-0.3 kg) all need more than 5.5 Hz.
- On three 21700 cells at 1.78 kg/m2: **0.54-0.66 kg, span 1.35-1.49 m,
  take-off at 4.0-4.4 Hz (margin 1.26-1.39 on frequency), 196-240 W for the
  seconds of the take-off**, which the three cells can supply about twice
  over (462 W at their continuous maximum). The cells hold 1.4-2.5 times the
  15 minutes at that mass.
- A pouch pack sized exactly removes the three-cell floor and gives 0.38-0.76
  kg at the same wing loading; it is not lighter per watt-hour.
- **The price is torque.** The cruise rule gives 6.7-9.1 N m at the shoulder
  for those spans, and the take-off, at 60% drive efficiency (TYPED), a mean
  of 9-12 N m. E-Flap's 71 g drive is estimated at 6.5 N m. So the drive is
  not E-Flap's with a new crank: it is a higher reduction of unknown mass
  (Phoenix's was designed for 17.7 N m; its mass was not found). The 14%
  drive fraction is therefore optimistic for exactly the rows that close.

## What L0 says, in one paragraph

Opening the power source did not move the bar; lowering the wing loading
does. A machine of about 0.55-0.65 kg on three 21700 cells with a span near
1.4 m can, on paper, fly 15 minutes and lift itself off the ground at about
4 Hz, at the cost of a shoulder torque of 9-12 N m that no drive in the
surveys is known to give at a known mass. The same slow, large wings swim at
a fraction of a hertz on that torque. Two rows do not close with one crank:
steering on land, and leaving the water, which is open and unprecedented and
depends on a stroke biased above the horizontal. Nothing here is a reason to
stop; the first things to measure are the drive (torque, speed range, mass)
and one wing pair's thrust on a stand.

## The reference machine for the first L1 pass (a fixture, not the design)

All-up 0.60 kg; three 21700; wing area 0.34 m2 in one pair, span 1.4 m,
1.78 kg/m2; a tail; crank drive limited to 12 N m and 5.5 Hz with a
torque-speed line; stroke 85 deg, biased upward by an amount L1 sweeps; hull
of 600 cm3 or more. Two pairs of the same total area is the second fixture
(decision 4). Predictions for each L1 measurement are written before it runs.

## Not checked by anyone

- The RoboFalcon calibration (lift coefficient 1.04, electrical peak 20 times
  ideal) is one tethered machine read from one paper, carried across wing
  loading by assumption.
- Structure at 20-30% and fixed mass at 80 g are typed. A 1.4 m wing at
  187 g/m2 is one built example's figure.
- The drive fraction ignores torque (above).
- Thrust-over-drag ratios for the runs use full-size hull data and another
  machine's in-flight thrust.
- Unquantised "cyl" rows are a bound, not a pack one can buy.
