# Pass S2, result: what holds the wing's twist

2026-11-17. Frozen text: `PREREG_S2_structure.md` (`ecb5fbc`). Run:
`s2/run_s2.py all` (CalculiX 2.23, about 25 s) -> `s2/results.jsonl` (45
rows); set-up decisions in `s2/NOTES.md`. Built and run by a subagent;
regenerated by me from the scripts (largest change in any number 4e-5) and
the tube's bending checked by hand (w L^4 / 8 E I = 24.4 mm against 24.28).

## Outcome in one paragraph

I predicted a thin cambered plate would be a twentieth as stiff in twist as
pass W7 asks. It is not: clamped at the root, an arc of 5.8% camber resists
twist mostly by the restraint of its warping, and reads ten times the
open-section value at 0.5 mm. **A 1.0 mm carbon plate alone has the
stiffness W7 asks (1.38 against 1.17 N m^2) but the pair of wings weighs
0.56 kg, the whole machine; at a weight the machine can carry (0.3 mm, 0.17
kg the pair) it has a quarter of it.** An 8x6 mm pultruded tube has it at
25 g a side, as predicted. Tube and thin plate together exceed it, at 0.22
kg the pair with a carbon plate and 0.14 kg with 6 mm depron.

Why I was wrong on S1, S3, S4: I took the open thin section's `c t^3 / 3`
for the whole answer. A curved strip clamped at one end cannot warp there,
and for an arc 258 mm wide and 15 mm high over 0.70 m that restraint is
most of the stiffness, along the whole length and not only near the root
(the outer half reads 0.38 where the open section is 0.049). On S5 I
guessed; the hand formula gives 24 mm.

An error in the frozen text: it wrote the load as "8.6 N/m^2 x 3" after
giving the weight on the wings as 17.3 N/m^2. Both were run; the table uses
17.3 x 3.

## What this means for the wing

- "A thin cambered plate" can be the whole wing only if it is thick enough
  to weigh as much as the machine. As built light, the plate carries the
  air load's shape and a spar carries the twist.
- With a spar that stiff, the twist the cruise needs (15 deg at the tip,
  40 at 6 m/s) cannot come from the structure giving way: at 1.0 N m the
  8x6 tube twists 32 deg, and W7's cruise torque is 0.55-0.69 N m. It has
  to be driven, by turning the plate about the spar or by turning the spar
  at its root. W7 flew the second (a tube with one root-to-tip torque) at
  7.2-7.7 W.
- How the plate is held at its root changes the stiffness by a third (1.71
  against 1.27). A plate free to turn on the spar and a plate bonded to a
  root rib are different wings.
- Mass: the fluid model's wing has no spar. Two 8x6 tubes and two 0.3 mm
  carbon plates are 0.22 kg of a 0.60 kg machine; with depron 0.14 kg. The
  mass budget of the flapper line has not carried either.

## Not a choice of construction (the user, 2026-11-17)

"太早選定構造了": material and construction are tuned after the mechanism's
structure is settled, not now. Nothing above selects a tube, a plate or a
foam. What the passes that follow take from this one is a range and two
sensitivities: a twist stiffness from 0.3 to 2.6 N m^2 is reachable at 18
to 110 g a side; the stiffness W7 asks is not reachable by a light plate
alone; and the wing's structural mass (0.05-0.22 kg the pair in these
cases) is not yet in the flapper's mass budget, so results are to be read
across that range.

## What these numbers rest on

Linear static; material values typical of hobby stock, assumed (the rows
carry GJ per unit G so another modulus can be put in); the sheet as an
orthotropic shell with Poisson's ratio 0.05; the tube and plate joined
along one line of nodes, which is a perfect bond of zero width; the foam
as a shell. Not done: buckling of the thin plate, flutter, fatigue, the
shoulder joint, the nonlinear twist of a soft plate. CalculiX would not
tie a beam's rotation to a shell with `*EQUATION` (no effect), so where
the tube carries a plate it is a thin shell of the tube's own area, J and
I.
