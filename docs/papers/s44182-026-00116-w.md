## 2. Problem (VERIFIED, Introduction)

How does butterfly wing shape affect flight performance, measured on a
controllable robot instead of live butterflies, and can a learned optimizer
pick wing shapes for a lift or a thrust objective? Platform: USTButterfly-II,
48.3 g, 57.5 cm span, one motor driving both wing pairs at up to 5 Hz through a
double-crank double-rocker (84° amplitude, ±42°), constant throttle, no
steering. Flexible wings: carbon/glass fibre veins, 0.03 mm CPE film, separate
fore- and hindwing.

## 3. Method

### 3.1 Three wing morphology parameters (WMPs) (VERIFIED, §Wing variation, Fig. 3)
- TC = L_T / L, convergence ratio of the forewing trailing edge (area distribution; moves r̂1, r̂2, barely AR).
- L, forewing characteristic length (span-like; AR rises with L).
- H, hindwing characteristic length (chord-like; AR falls with H).
- One factor at a time: 7 values per WMP around a shared baseline (TC 0.60, L 27 cm, H 27 cm), 19 wing pairs. Tolerances TC ±0.01, L/H ±2 mm. Wing mass "negligible" variation.

### 3.2 Ellington non-dimensional morphology (VERIFIED, Eq. 5, Fig. 3e, Fig. 4)
AR = R_w / c̄_w; r̂_k = (∫₀¹ ĉ_w r̂_w^k dr̂_w)^(1/k), k = 1, 2, with fore- and hindwing chords summed per span station. Same computed for 172 Papilionidae specimen images (binary-image pixel columns as strips); 36 fall in the robot wings' AR/r̂1/r̂2 range, 7 Parides species inside the 95% prediction interval of the r̂1–r̂2 regression.

### 3.3 Flight characteristic parameters (FCPs) (VERIFIED, Eq. 6-9)
Ascent angle θ = arctan(d_ver/d_hor) over the steady window; speed V = d_spa/t_F; flapping frequency f (from wing-tip markers); advance ratio V/f (Eq. 8, distance per cycle, dimensional); fore-hind phase offset δ = 2πf(t_HW − t_FW). Motion capture: 14 OptiTrack Prime 17W, 7 markers. Thrown take-off, constant throttle, five replicate flights per wing, battery within 0.1 V. Valid data: steady segment with > 3 body-oscillation cycles; straight only if yaw deviation within ±20°.

### 3.4 Trajectory decomposition (VERIFIED, Eq. 11-13, Fig. 6e-i)
Altitude = linear trend (b t + c) + body oscillation (a sin(w t − π/2)). The slope b carries the ascent angle; a and w the oscillation.

### 3.5 Abnormal-flight exclusion (VERIFIED, §Wing variation effect)
Stall: mean speed < 1.55 m/s. Fall: mean ascent angle < −5°. TC = 0.56 (stall) and L = 22.5 cm (fall) excluded; the falling wing had markedly the **highest** speed, frequency and advance ratio (Fig. 7, Fig. 8b-d). Sensitivity analysis: excluding them does not change the ANOVA significance pattern; it raises the TC–ascent correlation by Δr = 0.60.

### 3.7 DRL optimizer on a surrogate (VERIFIED, §DRL-based wing optimization, Fig. 9, Algorithms 1-3)
- Surrogate: feed-forward network, Bayesian regularisation (MacKay), WMPs → FCPs, trained on the 19 one-factor wings plus "cross-variant" wings (count UNKNOWN in main text), 89/11 split. r = 0.97 train, 0.9 test. Held-out physical wings (3): normalised RMSE of the five FCPs < 0.15 (Supp. Table 13).
- State s = [TC, L, H] in [0.6, 24, 25] .. [0.8, 31.5, 31]; bounds set by normal-flight envelope, manufacturability and the capture volume. 6 discrete actions: ±0.005 TC, ±0.1 L, ±0.1 H. DQN with target network, experience replay, ε-greedy decay.
- Objectives: L_p = θ_ascent / f (deg/Hz), T_p = V/f (m, the advance ratio).
- Multistep memory reward (Eq. 14, Alg. 2): r_t = mean of the newer half of a window of i past metric values minus mean of the older half. Best i: 16 (lift), 6 (thrust).
- Bound buffer (Alg. 3): when a step exceeds a bound, roll back only that coordinate and continue, up to a threshold count (best 10 lift, 5 thrust), then terminate.
- The two mechanisms together: +33% (lift), +11% (thrust) over the plain DRL control (Supp. Table 14). Converged after 160 / 120 episodes.

### 3.8 Optimised wings and flight test (VERIFIED, Fig. 10, §Optimization simulation and flight test)
- Lift: baseline [0.7, 27, 27] → [0.7, 31.5, 25.6]; L_p 2.23 → 9.8 (+339%), ascent 7.9° → 33.6°. Flight test 32.7°, error 1.6%.
- Thrust: → [0.8, 25.5, 27]; T_p 0.56 → 0.82 (+46%), speed 1.98 → 2.92 m/s. Flight test 3.1 m/s, error −6.6%.
- Against the best one-factor wing: +59% ascent (vs L = 31.5 cm, 20.6°), +19% speed (vs TC = 0.80, 2.6 m/s).
- Exhaustive search of the surrogate gives the global optimum [0.65, 31.5, 25] (lift) and [0.8, 25.3, 26.5] (thrust); both on the physical bounds. Bayesian optimisation reaches "comparable optimization performance" to the DRL (Supp. Fig. 1).
- Widened bounds [0.6, 24, 25] .. [0.9, 35, 31]: optima [0.7, 33.6, 25.4] and [0.85, 26, 27], interior; the authors flag the extension as not anchored by physical data.

## 4. Limitations (VERIFIED, Discussion)
One factor at a time, no interaction effects measured; scale prevents dynamic similarity with real butterflies; findings are "testable hypotheses". Three parameters, 3-D state space small enough for exhaustive search. INFERRED: the DRL is a sequential local search on a fixed surrogate; with BO matching it and exhaustive search possible, the DRL is not shown to be needed at this dimension.
